Twelve months ago RLHF debates were "PPO vs. DPO." Today the landscape is
both richer and harder to summarize. This post organizes the recent work
into three threads — **DPO and its descendants**, **on-policy revival**, and
**reward-model rethinking** — and notes what each thread implies for
practitioners.

## Thread 1: DPO and its descendants

DPO {% cite rafailov2023direct %} kicked off a family of offline,
preference-only methods. The follow-ups vary along three axes:

- **Loss shape**: replacing the logistic with hinge, identity, or
  KL-regularized variants to control reward saturation.
- **Reference policy**: using a moving reference, an SFT-only reference, or
  no reference at all.
- **Data construction**: pairwise vs. listwise, on-policy resampling, and
  hybrid teacher/critic data.

The empirical lesson: most DPO variants are within noise of each other on
pairwise win-rate benchmarks, but they diverge on calibration and on
out-of-distribution prompts.

## Thread 2: The on-policy revival

After a year of "DPO replaces PPO" claims, the pendulum has swung back. PPO
{% cite schulman2017proximal %} and its REINFORCE-style cousins are
re-appearing in two contexts:

1. **Reasoning training**, where the reward signal is a verifier (correctness
   on math/code) rather than a learned RM, and the variance reduction PPO
   provides matters.
2. **Iterated preference collection**, where on-policy samples from the
   current model are scored by a reward model — recovering the InstructGPT
   recipe {% cite ouyang2022training %} but with much better RMs.

## Thread 3: Reward models, again

The early RLHF era treated reward models as commodity. The current era
treats them as the bottleneck. Three open problems:

- **Generalization** beyond the preference distribution they were trained
  on, especially under distribution shift between SFT and RLHF stages.
- **Reward hacking** — both Goodhart-style ("the policy gets good at the
  metric") and exploit-style ("the policy finds an out-of-distribution
  region the RM rates highly").
- **Constitutional / RL-from-AI-feedback** approaches as in
  {% cite bai2022training %}, which trade human label cost for new failure
  modes around the AI labeler's biases.

## Practitioner takeaways

| If you have... | Start with... |
|---|---|
| Static preference dataset, limited compute | DPO or a stable variant |
| A verifier (unit tests, exact match) | On-policy PPO/REINFORCE |
| Budget for iterated data collection | RM + on-policy rounds |
| Concerns about reward hacking | Smaller KL budget, careful eval suite |

## Where I think the next year goes

Two predictions, one weak and one strong:

- *(weak)* The DPO-vs-PPO question dissolves into "what is your reward
  signal and is it on-policy or off-policy?", which was always the real
  question.
- *(strong)* Reward-model evaluation methodology will get more attention
  than preference-method papers in 2026 — that is where the field is
  bottlenecked.
